Film stress and wafer bow

A film a few hundred nanometres thick can bend a half-millimetre wafer by tens of micrometres. Where does the stress come from, how big is the bow – and how do you measure it?

Key equations

Total stress = intrinsic (growth) stress + thermal-mismatch stress from cooling down from the deposition temperature (tension positive):

\sigma = \sigma_{int} + \sigma_{th}, \qquad \sigma_{th} = \frac{E_f}{1-\nu_f}\,(\alpha_s - \alpha_f)\,\Delta T, \quad \Delta T = T - T_{dep}

Stoney equation for a film of thickness h_f on a much thicker substrate (h_s, biaxial modulus M_s = E_s/(1-\nu_s)), and the bow of a wafer of radius r:

\frac{1}{R} = \frac{6\,\sigma\,h_f}{M_s\,h_s^2}, \qquad \delta = \frac{r^2}{2R}

Try it yourself

  1. 200 nm of LPCVD nitride with +1 GPa on a 100 mm wafer (525 µm). Radius of curvature and bow?
    Answer1/R = 6\cdot10^9\cdot0.2\times10^{-6}/(180\times10^9\cdot(525\times10^{-6})^2) = 0.0242\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}, R \approx 41\ \mathrm{m}; \delta = 0.05^2/(2\cdot41) \approx 30\ \mu\mathrm{m}, concave.
  2. After depositing 500 nm of a film on a 150 mm wafer (675 µm) the laser scan shows a convex curvature with R = 40\ \mathrm{m} (the wafer was flat before). What is the film stress?
    Answer\sigma = M_s h_s^2/(6 h_f R) = 180\times10^9\cdot(675\times10^{-6})^2/(6\cdot0.5\times10^{-6}\cdot40) \approx 680\ \mathrm{MPa}, compressive (convex). The film's own elastic constants are not needed.
  3. An Al film (E = 70.3 GPa, ν = 0.345, α = 23.1 ppm/K) is stress-free at 400 °C. What thermal stress does it reach at 25 °C if it stays elastic? The mean α of Si between 25 and 400 °C is 3.45 ppm/K.
    Answer\sigma_{th} = \frac{70.3}{0.655}\,\mathrm{GPa}\cdot(3.45 - 23.1)\times10^{-6}\cdot(25 - 400) \approx +790\ \mathrm{MPa}, tensile – far above what a thin Al film withstands, so in reality it yields plastically. That is why metal stress depends on the whole thermal history.

Model & limitations

  • Stoney equation: thin film (h_f \ll h_s), uniform equal-biaxial stress, small deflection, isotropic in-plane substrate stiffness (true for Si(100), not for Si(111) or other cuts).
  • Thermal stress is purely elastic: no plastic yield, creep or viscous flow, so metal films after hot steps and oxides at very high temperatures are overestimated. The deposition temperature is taken as the stress-free reference for the thermal part.
  • Film data are bulk values: fused quartz for thermal SiO₂, bulk silicon-nitride ceramic for the expansion of nitride films, bulk metals. Real film properties differ, so the split into intrinsic and thermal stress for the nitrides (default intrinsic = measured total − computed thermal) is approximate. For thermal SiO₂ the computed thermal stress (−366 MPa from 1200 °C) is close to the measured −310 MPa, so \sigma_{int} = 0 is used.
  • Intrinsic stress depends strongly on the process (temperature, pressure, gas ratios, ion bombardment) – the defaults are single literature values, not tool data.
  • The bow is drawn exaggerated; the laser beams of the measurement sketch are schematic.
