Carriers and the pn junction

Every doping step in microfabrication ultimately makes junctions. How many carriers does silicon have, where is the Fermi level – and what happens where p meets n?

Key equations

Carriers in a uniformly doped region (full ionisation, non-degenerate):

n\,p = n_i^2, \qquad n = \frac{N_D - N_A}{2} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{N_D - N_A}{2}\right)^2 + n_i^2}, \qquad E_F - E_i = kT\ln\frac{n}{n_i}

Abrupt pn junction in the depletion approximation (V forward positive):

V_{bi} = \frac{kT}{q}\ln\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}, \qquad W = \sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_s (V_{bi}-V)}{q}\,\frac{N_A + N_D}{N_A N_D}}
N_A x_p = N_D x_n, \qquad E_{max} = \frac{2\,(V_{bi}-V)}{W}, \qquad C_j = \frac{\varepsilon_s}{W}

Try it yourself

  1. A junction with N_A = 10^{17} and N_D = 10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}} at 300 K: built-in voltage, depletion width and its split, maximum field?
    AnswerV_{bi} = 0.02585\cdot\ln(10^{33}/(9.70\times10^{9})^2) = 0.775\ \mathrm{V}. W = 0.332\ \mu\mathrm{m}, of which x_n = 0.302 and x_p = 0.030\ \mu\mathrm{m} (ratio N_A/N_D = 10). E_{max} = 2\cdot0.775/0.332\ \mu\mathrm{m} = 46.7\ \mathrm{kV/cm}.
  2. A p⁺n diode (N_A = 10^{19}, N_D = 10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}) of 100 µm × 100 µm at −5 V: depletion width and capacitance?
    AnswerOne-sided: W \approx \sqrt{2\varepsilon_s (V_{bi} - V)/(qN_D)} with V_{bi} = 0.835\ \mathrm{V}: W = 2.75\ \mu\mathrm{m}. C = \varepsilon_s A/W = 3.77\ \mathrm{nF/cm^2}\cdot10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm^2} \approx 0.38\ \mathrm{pF}.
  3. By how much does n_i grow from 300 K to 400 K, and what happens to V_{bi} of the junction in task 1?
    Answern_i(400\ \mathrm{K}) = 5.5\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}} – about 560× more. V_{bi} falls from 0.775 to 0.597 V: kT grows, but \ln(N_AN_D/n_i^2) shrinks much faster.

Model & limitations

  • Full ionisation of dopants and Boltzmann statistics. Above roughly 10¹⁸ cm⁻³ the Fermi level approaches the band edge (degeneracy), the ionisation energies shrink and band-gap narrowing sets in; at low temperature dopants freeze out. None of this is modelled.
  • Abrupt junction with uniform doping on both sides, depletion approximation (no free carriers inside W, none outside). It fails close to V = V_{bi} in forward bias. Diffused and implanted junctions are graded, not abrupt.
  • The intrinsic level is drawn at mid-gap (it lies within about 10 meV of it); the quasi-Fermi levels under bias are drawn schematically through the depletion region.
  • n_i(T) is the Misiakos–Tsamakis fit, measured from 78 to 340 K; above 340 K it is an extrapolation that agrees with newer fits (Couderc et al. 2014) within a few per cent up to 700 K.
  • The breakdown voltage is shown as the range between two published approximations for a one-sided abrupt junction (about 15 % apart); real breakdown depends on junction curvature, edge termination and temperature. The fits describe avalanche multiplication only: below about 4Eg/q ≈ 4.5 V a junction breaks down by tunnelling (Zener breakdown) instead (Sze & Ng, ch. 2), and no value is shown.
