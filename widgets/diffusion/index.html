Two-step diffusion and junction depth

Predeposition sets the dose, drive-in sets the depth. Where does the junction end up, and what is its sheet resistance?

Key equations

Fick's second law with a constant diffusivity, \partial C/\partial t = D\,\partial^2 C/\partial x^2, with an Arrhenius diffusivity D = D_0\,e^{-E_a/kT}.

Predeposition – constant surface concentration

C(x) = C_s\,\mathrm{erfc}\frac{x}{2\sqrt{D_1t_1}}, \qquad Q = 2C_s\sqrt{\frac{D_1t_1}{\pi}}

Drive-in – constant dose

C(x) \approx \frac{Q}{\sqrt{\pi D_2t_2}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^2}{4D_2t_2}\right) \quad (D_2t_2 \gg D_1t_1)

Junction depth, sheet resistance

x_j = 2\sqrt{D_1t_1}\,\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}\frac{N_B}{C_s}, \qquad x_j = \sqrt{4D_2t_2\,\ln\frac{Q}{N_B\sqrt{\pi D_2t_2}}}
R_s = \frac{1}{q\int_0^{x_j}\mu(C)\,\left[C(x)-N_B\right]dx} \;\approx\; \frac{1}{q\,\bar{\mu}\,Q}

The right-hand form with an average mobility \bar\mu is the quick estimate; the widget evaluates the integral. A four-point probe measures R_s = \frac{\pi}{\ln 2}\,\frac{V}{I} \approx 4.53\,\frac{V}{I} (thin layer, probes far from the edges).

Try it yourself

  1. Boron predeposition at 950 °C for 30 min into an n-type wafer with N_B = 10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}, C_s = 1.55\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}, D = 3.8\times10^{-15}\ \mathrm{cm^2/s}. Calculate the dose and the junction depth.
    Answer\sqrt{Dt} = \sqrt{3.8\times10^{-15}\cdot1800} = 2.6\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{cm}. Q = 2\cdot1.55\times10^{20}\cdot2.6\times10^{-6}/\sqrt{\pi} \approx 4.6\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}}. \mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(6.5\times10^{-6}) \approx 3.19, so x_j \approx 2\cdot0.026\cdot3.19 \approx 0.17\ \mathrm{\mu m}.
  2. The same dose is then driven in for 60 min at 1100 °C (D = 1.43\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm{cm^2/s}). Estimate x_j with the Gaussian formula, then compare with the simulation.
    AnswerD_2t_2 = 5.1\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{cm^2}, \sqrt{\pi D_2t_2} = 4.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{cm}, \ln\frac{4.6\times10^{14}}{10^{15}\cdot4.0\times10^{-5}} \approx 9.3, x_j \approx \sqrt{4\cdot5.1\times10^{-10}\cdot9.3} \approx 1.4\ \mathrm{\mu m}. The exact solution in the simulation gives 1.39 µm.
  3. Raise the drive-in temperature from 1100 °C to 1150 °C. By what factor does D grow, and by what factor does the junction depth grow? Why are the factors different?
    AnswerD grows ≈ 2.8× (boron), x_j roughly with \sqrt{D}, i.e. ≈ 1.6–1.7× (the logarithm in the formula changes slightly as well).
  4. Switch to phosphorus. Why does the readout warn that C_s > n_i, and what would a real profile look like?
    AnswerAt 950 °C, n_i \approx 5\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}} – far below C_s. In extrinsic material the diffusivity depends on the electron concentration, and P shows the “kink-and-tail” profile: a flat, highly doped surface region and a fast-diffusing tail. The constant-D model is only a first estimate.

Model & limitations

  • Tabulated diffusivities differ between textbooks and course notes – for boron by up to about 1.6× (e.g. 0.9–1.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ cm²/s at 1000 °C, depending on the prefactor of the D⁺ term). This page uses Plummer et al. (2000).
  • Constant, intrinsic diffusivity. At surface concentrations above n_i(T) (practically always during a solid-solubility predeposition) real B, P and As diffuse faster and with concentration-dependent profiles; oxidation during drive-in (OED) speeds boron and phosphorus up further.
  • The drive-in surface is sealed (no out-diffusion, no segregation into a growing oxide). The drive-in profile is calculated exactly from the erfc predeposit by the method of images; the Gaussian is only drawn for comparison.
  • Predeposition holds the surface at the maximum total solid solubility. For phosphorus the electrically active limit is only a few 10²⁰ cm⁻³; sheet resistances of heavy P diffusions are therefore underestimated.
  • Sheet resistance: Arora's mobility model at 300 K, full ionisation, mobility evaluated at the total impurity concentration C + N_B.
  • The intrinsic carrier density used for the C_s > n_i warning follows Morin & Maita (1954), whose fit is stated for temperatures below 700 K; at diffusion temperatures it is an extrapolation (the classic diffusion models were calibrated with it).
  • The lateral spread under the mask edge (≈ 0.8 x_j) is a commonly quoted rule of thumb from 2-D analyses of diffusion at a mask edge (Kennedy & O’Brien 1965), drawn schematically.
