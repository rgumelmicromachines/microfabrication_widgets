Film colour and reflectometry

Why does a transparent film make a wafer blue, gold or pink – and how does the same effect measure its thickness to a nanometre?

Key equations

A film of index n_1 and thickness d on silicon reflects light at its top and at its bottom. The wave reflected at the bottom is delayed by the phase 2\beta, and all multiple reflections add up to (Airy formula)

r = \frac{r_{01} + r_{12}\,e^{2i\beta}}{1 + r_{01}\,r_{12}\,e^{2i\beta}}, \qquad \beta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,n_1 d\cos\theta_1, \qquad R = |r|^2

with the Fresnel coefficients of the two interfaces, at normal incidence r_{ij} = (N_i - N_j)/(N_i + N_j), and Snell’s law \sin\theta_0 = n_1\sin\theta_1. For air < film < silicon both reflections flip the phase, so

2\,n_1 d\cos\theta_1 = \left(m + \tfrac12\right)\lambda \;\Rightarrow\; \text{minimum}, \qquad 2\,n_1 d\cos\theta_1 = m\,\lambda \;\Rightarrow\; \text{maximum} \;(R = R_{\text{bare Si}})

The same phase returns when the film is thicker by \Delta d = \lambda/(2 n_1\cos\theta_1). A quarter-wave film, d = \lambda/(4 n_1), gives the first minimum; it is exactly zero when n_1^2 = n_0\,n_2 (antireflection coating).

Two neighbouring minima at \lambda_1 > \lambda_2 differ by one order, which gives the thickness without knowing the order (normal incidence, n_i = n_1(\lambda_i)):

d = \frac{\lambda_1\,\lambda_2}{2\,(n_2\lambda_1 - n_1\lambda_2)}

Try it yourself

  1. What is the thinnest SiO₂ film with a reflectance minimum at 550 nm, and the next one? Check both with the thickness slider.
    Answerd = \lambda/(4n_1) = 550/(4\cdot1.460) = 94\ \mathrm{nm} (m = 0), then 3\lambda/(4n_1) = 283\ \mathrm{nm} (m = 1). Their difference is \lambda/(2n_1) = 188\ \mathrm{nm}.
  2. The reflectance spectrum of an oxide film has neighbouring minima at 647 nm and 531 nm. How thick is the film?
    AnswerWith a constant index, d = \lambda_1\lambda_2/[2n_1(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)] = 647\cdot531/(2\cdot1.46\cdot116) \approx 1014\ \mathrm{nm}. With the dispersion (n = 1.457 at 647 nm, 1.461 at 531 nm) the formula above gives 1000 nm: the small change of the index between the two minima matters, because the denominator is a difference of two nearly equal numbers.
  3. A wafer with 300 nm of oxide is tilted to 60°. Which way does the colour shift, and which thickness would look similar at normal incidence?
    Answer\sin\theta_1 = \sin 60°/1.46, so \theta_1 = 36.4° and \cos\theta_1 = 0.805: the path difference is that of a 300\cdot0.805 \approx 241\ \mathrm{nm} film. The minimum moves from 583 nm to 472 nm, towards the blue. (The match is not exact: at 60° the two polarisations reflect differently.)
  4. Which nitride thickness gives the lowest reflection at 600 nm, and why is it so much better than an oxide film?
    Answerd = 600/(4\cdot2.015) = 74\ \mathrm{nm}. The minimum is zero when n_1^2 = n_{Si}: for nitride 2.015^2 = 4.06 against n_{Si} = 3.94 – almost perfect, R ≈ 0.02 % instead of 35 % for bare silicon. A quarter-wave oxide (1.458^2 = 2.13) still reflects 9 %.

Model & limitations

  • Ideal, flat, homogeneous, non-absorbing film on thick silicon; coherent, unpolarised light. Roughness, graded or absorbing films (silicon-rich nitride, polysilicon, dyed resist) and film stacks need a full multilayer calculation.
  • Optical constants are those of fused silica and of stoichiometric CVD nitride. Deposited films differ: PECVD nitride and oxide vary in index with composition and density, which shifts the colours. Silicon data are tabulated every 50 nm between 400 and 700 nm and interpolated; outside this range the end values are used, which affects only the faint violet and deep-red ends of the colour calculation.
  • Colour: CIE 1931 standard observer, daylight (a 6504 K black body), converted to sRGB and brightened by a fixed factor as a camera exposure would. Real impressions depend on the lamp, on the surroundings and on your display.
  • Viewing angle: the same angle of incidence is assumed for the whole wafer (a distant observer and light source). The outline of the wafer is foreshortened by cos θ₀.
  • The ray sketch is schematic (film thickness not to scale; at normal incidence the rays are drawn slightly tilted so that they can be told apart). The colour strips and the spectrum are computed.
