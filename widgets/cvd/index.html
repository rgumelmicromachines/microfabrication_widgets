CVD growth rate: reaction or transport?

Two steps in series – the gas must bring the precursor to the wafer, and the surface must react it. The slower one sets the growth rate.

Key equations (Grove model)

Flux through the stagnant boundary layer of thickness \delta and flux consumed by the surface reaction:

F_1 = h_G\,(C_G - C_S),\quad h_G = \frac{D_G}{\delta}, \qquad F_2 = k_S\,C_S

In steady state F_1 = F_2, which gives the surface concentration and the growth rate

C_S = \frac{C_G}{1 + k_S/h_G}, \qquad V = \frac{F}{N_1} = \frac{k_S\,h_G}{k_S + h_G}\,\frac{C_T}{N_1}\,Y

with C_T the density of gas molecules, Y the mole fraction of the precursor and N_1 = 5\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}} the density of Si atoms in the film. The limits:

h_G \gg k_S:\ V \simeq \frac{C_T}{N_1}k_S Y\ \text{(reaction)}, \qquad h_G \ll k_S:\ V \simeq \frac{C_T}{N_1}h_G Y\ \text{(transport)}
k_S = k_0\,e^{-E_a/kT}, \qquad h_G = \frac{3}{2}\,\frac{D_G}{L}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_L}, \qquad T^* = \frac{E_a}{k\,\ln(k_0/h_G)}\ \ (k_S = h_G)

Try it yourself

  1. At which temperature do the two regimes meet for h_G = 5\ \mathrm{cm/s}? Calculate, then check in Explore mode.
    AnswerT^* = 1.9/(8.617\times10^{-5}\cdot\ln(10^7/5)) = 1519.7\ \mathrm{K} \approx 1247\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}.
  2. Growth at 1100 °C with h_G = 5\ \mathrm{cm/s} and Y = 0.5\ \%: what is k_S, the growth rate, and how long does a 10 µm epitaxial layer take?
    Answerk_S = 1.06\ \mathrm{cm/s}, C_T = 5.35\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}, C_G = 2.67\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}; V = \frac{1.06\cdot5}{6.06}\cdot\frac{2.67\times10^{16}}{5\times10^{22}} = 4.7\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{cm/s} = 0.28\ \mu\mathrm{m/min}. 10 µm takes about 36 min. The process is reaction-limited (k_S/h_G = 0.21).
  3. In the reaction-limited regime around 900 °C, how much does the growth rate change per 1 °C? What does this mean for a furnace with a ±2 °C temperature spread?
    Answer\mathrm{d}\ln V/\mathrm{d}T = E_a/kT^2 - 1/T = 0.0160 - 0.0009 \approx 1.5\ \%/^\circ\mathrm{C} (the -1/T comes from C_T = p/kT). A ±2 °C spread gives about ±3 % thickness spread – temperature uniformity is what matters in LPCVD.
  4. At 1320 °C, h_G = 2\ \mathrm{cm/s}, the gas velocity is doubled. By how much does the growth rate increase?
    Answerh_G \propto \sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_L} \propto \sqrt{U}, so h_G grows 1.41×. The rate grows only 1.32×: with k_S = 9.8\ \mathrm{cm/s} the surface reaction still contributes (1/V \propto 1/k_S + 1/h_G). Only in the pure transport limit would the gain be the full 1.41×.

Model & limitations

  • Stagnant-film model: one precursor, a first-order surface reaction and no gas-phase reactions. The rate constants are those of SiCl₄ + H₂ epitaxy; other precursors (SiH₄, SiH₂Cl₂, TEOS …) have their own k_0 and E_a.
  • h_G is a free parameter and is taken as independent of temperature, as in Grove’s figure. In reality D_G grows with temperature, so the transport-limited rate rises slightly with T; here it even falls slightly, because C_T = p/kT decreases.
  • The rate is proportional to Y only for dilute precursors. For SiCl₄ the measured rate peaks near Y \approx 0.1 and turns into etching above Y \approx 0.28 (HCl from the reaction etches Si; Theuerer 1961). Typical industrial growth uses Y \approx 0.01.
  • Pressure is fixed at 1 atm (APCVD / atmospheric epitaxy). At low pressure (LPCVD) D_G \propto 1/p; the boundary layer also thickens (\delta \propto 1/\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}} \propto p^{-1/2}), so net h_G \propto p^{-1/2} still grows strongly as the pressure drops – LPCVD stays reaction-limited.
  • The cross-section is schematic: the drawn boundary-layer thickness only indicates \delta = D_G/h_G qualitatively, and the concentration profile is the linear stagnant-film profile.
