Crystal growth

Dopant segregation in Czochralski growth

Why the two ends of a doped silicon ingot never have the same resistivity.

Key equations

The equilibrium segregation coefficient is the ratio of the dopant concentration the solid accepts to the concentration in the liquid it grows from:

k_0 = \frac{C_s}{C_L}

For a well-mixed melt of initial concentration C_0 and negligible diffusion in the solid, a mass balance gives the Scheil (normal-freezing) equation, where f is the fraction of the melt already solidified:

C_s(f) = k_0\,C_0\,(1-f)^{k_0-1}, \qquad C_L(f) = C_0\,(1-f)^{k_0-1}

The amount of dopant still in the melt is (1-f)\,C_L/C_0 = (1-f)^{k_0} of the starting amount.

Try it yourself

  1. A customer accepts wafers whose doping is at most 20 % above the seed-end value. Which fraction of a phosphorus-doped ingot qualifies? And of a boron-doped one?
    AnswerSolve (1-f)^{k_0-1} = 1.2: f = 1 - 1.2^{1/(k_0-1)}. Phosphorus: f ≈ 0.24 (only the first quarter). Boron: f ≈ 0.60.
  2. The seed end of a boron-doped ingot has 1.0 × 10¹⁵ cm⁻³. What concentration do wafers cut at f = 0.9 have?
    Answer10^{15}\cdot 0.1^{-0.2} \approx 1.6\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}
  3. Set f = 0.5 in Explore mode. Why is the gap between the yellow and teal curves the same for every f, but different for each dopant?
    AnswerOn a log axis, \log C_s - \log C_L = \log k_0 – a constant that depends only on the dopant.

Model & limitations

  • The melt is assumed perfectly mixed. In a real puller a stagnant boundary layer at the interface makes the effective coefficient k_\mathrm{eff} lie between k_0 and 1 (Burton–Prim–Slichter), so the real axial variation is somewhat weaker.
  • Evaporation from the melt (important for Sb and As) and dopant pick-up from the crucible are neglected.
  • k_0 is taken as concentration-independent – valid for the dilute doping of substrate wafers.
  • The drawing of the crucible and ingot is schematic; the colour of each slice follows its computed concentration on a log scale.
