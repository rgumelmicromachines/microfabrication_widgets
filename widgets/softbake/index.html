Soft bake

A minute on a hotplate between coating and exposure. What does it remove, why does longer not help, and why not simply bake hotter?

Key equations

After spin coating the film still contains casting solvent, here w_0 \approx 28 wt%. During the bake the solvent diffuses to the surface and evaporates. Measurements on a hotplate are described by the desorption of a slab of thickness l towards a plateau that depends on the temperature:

w(t) = w_0 - M_\infty(T)\left[1 - \frac{8}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+1)^2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(2n+1)^2\pi^2 D\,t}{4\,l^2}\right)\right]
Bake temperature70 °C90 °C110 °C
D (cm²/s)8.22 × 10⁻¹¹1.32 × 10⁻¹⁰1.94 × 10⁻¹⁰
M∞ (wt%)6.8412.316.3
plateau w0 − M∞ (wt%)21.215.711.7

Gardiner et al. (1997): PGMEA in a 1.15 µm novolak film with 12 wt% DNQ, vacuum hotplate; D follows an activation energy of 5.62 kcal/mol. The law is an empirical fit for bake times up to about ten minutes.

The photoactive compound (PAC) of a DNQ resist decomposes thermally with first-order kinetics; m' is the fraction left:

m' = e^{-K_T\,t}, \qquad K_T = A_r\,e^{-E_a/RT}, \qquad E_a = 30.3\ \mathrm{kcal/mol},\quad \ln A_r = 35.3\ \ (A_r \text{ in min}^{-1})

Mack & Carback (1985), Kodak 820 resist. In a convection oven t is replaced by t - t_{wup}, the time after the substrate has warmed up.

Film shrinkage from a mass balance (volumes of resin and solvent additive, equal densities): L/L_0 = (1 - w_0)/(1 - w) with the weight fractions w_0, w.

Try it yourself

  1. How much solvent is left after 60 s on a hotplate at 100 °C, and how much thinner is the film than after spinning?
    AnswerAbout 15.6 wt%. L/L_0 = (1-0.28)/(1-0.156) = 0.853: the film is about 15 % thinner.
  2. Which fraction of the photoactive compound is lost in a 30 min oven bake at 100 °C (ignore the warm-up), and in a 1 min hotplate bake at the same temperature?
    AnswerK_T = \exp[35.3 - 30.3/(1.987\times10^{-3}\cdot373.15)] = 3.8\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{min^{-1}}. Oven: 1 - e^{-0.115} = 11\ \%. Hotplate: 0.38 %. The long oven bake costs thirty times more.
  3. By which factor does the decomposition speed up between 100 °C and 110 °C?
    Answer\exp[(E_a/R)(1/373.15 - 1/383.15)] = \exp(1.07) \approx 2.9 – it almost triples every 10 °C, while the solvent plateau only moves from 13.7 to 11.7 wt%.
  4. A film is baked at 70 °C. Would 10 min instead of 1 min bring it to the solvent content of a 1 min bake at 100 °C?
    AnswerNo. At 70 °C the plateau is 21.2 wt%; the film never gets below it in a practical time, however long the bake. 100 °C reaches 15.6 wt% in one minute. The temperature sets how dry the film can get, the time only how close it comes to that value.

Model & limitations

  • The solvent law is the empirical fit of one study to one resist system (PGMEA in a 1.15 µm novolak/DNQ film) at 70, 90 and 110 °C for bake times up to about ten minutes. Between these temperatures the plateau is interpolated linearly; below 70 °C and above 110 °C the page extrapolates and says so. The film thickness is fixed at that of the measurement; thicker films need longer.
  • Published solvent contents differ widely: this dataset finds 12–18 wt% after one to two minutes at 90–110 °C, while resist suppliers quote a few per cent after a typical bake (MicroChemicals; different resists, units and methods). Use the page for the trends – fast drying, a temperature-dependent plateau – not for absolute numbers of another resist.
  • The decomposition parameters are those of one resist (Kodak 820, from oven bakes at 80–125 °C); other DNQ resists differ, and chemically amplified resists do not contain DNQ at all. No threshold for an “acceptable” loss is implied.
  • Hotplate: the wafer is taken to be at the plate temperature from the start (it gets there within a few seconds). Oven: the film is treated as cold during the warm-up time and at the set temperature afterwards; real ovens heat gradually and unevenly.
  • Thickness: mass balance with additive volumes and equal densities of resin and solvent – an estimate, not a measurement.
  • The effect of the residual solvent on the development rate is not modelled. The picture is schematic; only the film thickness relative to the as-spun film is drawn to scale.
