Stress migration

A metal line can break while the chip just sits on a shelf. Why is there a worst temperature – and why is a hotter test not a faster one?

Key equations

A metal line enclosed in its dielectric cannot shrink freely when it cools, because the metal contracts much more than the silicon and the dielectric around it. Below the stress-free temperature T_0 it is under a tensile stress \sigma \propto T_0 - T. The stress makes vacancies collect into voids at the creep rate (McPherson & Dunn)

R(T) = C\,(T_0 - T)^{N}\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a}{kT}\right), \qquad TTF = C_0\,(T_0 - T)^{-N}\exp\!\left(\frac{E_a}{kT}\right)

with the creep exponent N and the activation energy E_a of the diffusion that feeds the voids. The stress factor falls and the diffusion factor rises with temperature, so the rate has a maximum at

\frac{N}{T_0 - T_{max}} = \frac{E_a}{k\,T_{max}^2} \qquad (T \text{ in kelvin})

An Arrhenius plot of measured failure times shows only an apparent activation energy,

E_{eff} = E_a - \frac{N\,k\,T^2}{T_0 - T}

which is smaller than E_a, zero at T_{max} and negative above it. The acceleration of a storage test at T against use at T_{use} is AF = R(T)/R(T_{use}).

Try it yourself

  1. Check that 190 °C is the temperature of fastest voiding for the copper parameters.
    AnswerAt 463.15 K: N/(T_0 - T) = 3.2/80 = 0.040\ \mathrm{K^{-1}} and E_a/kT^2 = 0.74/(8.617\times10^{-5}\cdot463.15^2) = 0.040\ \mathrm{K^{-1}} – equal, so the two factors change at the same relative rate in opposite directions.
  2. How much faster does a copper structure void in a bake at 190 °C than in use at 105 °C?
    AnswerStress factor: (80/165)^{3.2} = 0.099. Diffusion factor: \exp[(0.74/k)(1/378.15 - 1/463.15)] = 64.5. AF = 0.099\cdot64.5 \approx 6.4. A thousand hours in the oven stand for only about nine months of use.
  3. Someone proposes to bake at 250 °C “to speed things up”. What happens?
    AnswerOnly 20 K below T_0 the stress factor collapses: (20/165)^{3.2} = 0.0012, while diffusion gains a factor 541. AF \approx 0.63 – the test runs slower than the product in use and proves nothing.
  4. Which apparent activation energy would an Arrhenius plot of Al–Si failure times show around 105 °C?
    AnswerE_{eff} = 0.58 - 2.33\cdot8.617\times10^{-5}\cdot378.15^2/127 = 0.58 - 0.23 = 0.35\ \mathrm{eV} – well below the 0.58 eV of the diffusion itself. Activation energies read from stress-migration tests depend on the temperature range of the test.

Model & limitations

  • The model gives the temperature dependence only. The prefactor – and with it any absolute lifetime – depends on the geometry, the microstructure and the process; rates and times are therefore shown relative to their extreme value.
  • The parameter sets are fits to two specific structures (a via on a wide copper lead; 3 µm Al–Si lines). The aluminium values are taken from a later paper that quotes the original study. Other line widths, alloys, barriers and dielectrics give other values; narrow copper lines with vias showed no significant voiding in the same study.
  • The stress is taken as proportional to T0 − T with a single stress-free temperature. Real lines relax part of their stress by plastic flow and creep during the test, and voids relieve the stress around them; the model has no time dependence of the stress.
  • No current flows in this test. In a product, electromigration and stress migration act together.
  • The picture is schematic: void shape and position, grain boundaries, vacancies and the length of the stress arrows only illustrate the two factors. The plots are computed.
