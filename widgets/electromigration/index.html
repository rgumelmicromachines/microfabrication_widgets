Electromigration

The current itself wears out a metal line. How does a stress test of a few days predict a lifetime of ten years?

Key equations

The conduction electrons transfer momentum to the metal atoms (“electron wind”) and move them towards the anode. The median time to failure of a line follows Black’s equation

t_{50} = A\,j^{-n}\exp\!\left(\frac{E_a}{kT}\right)

with the current density j, the activation energy E_a of the fastest diffusion path and an exponent n \approx 2 when the nucleation of a void takes most of the time, n \approx 1 when its growth does. The prefactor A depends on the film and the geometry, so lifetimes are measured in an accelerated test and scaled with the acceleration factor

AF = \frac{t_{50,\,use}}{t_{50,\,test}} = \left(\frac{j_{test}}{j_{use}}\right)^{\!n}\exp\!\left[\frac{E_a}{k}\left(\frac{1}{T_{use}}-\frac{1}{T_{test}}\right)\right]

Failure times are lognormally distributed, F(t) = \Phi\!\left[\ln(t/t_{50})/\sigma\right], so a fraction F has failed at

t_F = t_{50}\,e^{\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(F)}, \qquad t_{0.1\,\%} = t_{50}\,e^{-3.09\,\sigma}

Blech effect: the stress built up by the displaced atoms drives them back. A line does not fail when

j\,L < (j\,L)_c = \frac{\Omega\,\Delta\sigma}{Z^{*}e\,\rho}

(\Omega atomic volume, \Delta\sigma the stress difference the line can sustain, Z^{*}e effective charge, \rho resistivity).

These numbers describe the films of the cited studies. The activation energy follows the fastest path: grain boundaries in polycrystalline aluminium, the capped top interface in damascene copper, the lattice only when all faster paths are blocked (1.48 eV for aluminium, as quoted by Black).

Try it yourself

  1. A large-grained aluminium line (Ea = 0.84 eV, n = 2) has a median life of 100 h at 2 MA/cm² and 250 °C. What is the acceleration factor to 0.2 MA/cm² and 125 °C, and the median life in use?
    AnswerCurrent: (2/0.2)^2 = 100. Temperature: \exp[(0.84/8.617\times10^{-5})(1/398.15 - 1/523.15)] = 347. AF = 3.47\times10^{4}, so t_{50} = 3.47\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{h} \approx 396 years.
  2. With σ = 0.5, when have 0.1 % of these lines failed? Is a 10-year requirement met, and up to which current density?
    Answert_{0.1\,\%} = 396\cdot e^{-3.09\cdot0.5} = 396\cdot0.213 \approx 84 years – met. The life scales as j^{-2}, so the limit is j_{max} = 0.2\cdot\sqrt{84/10} \approx 0.58\ \mathrm{MA/cm^2}.
  3. The same test result is obtained on a fine-grained film with Ea = 0.48 eV. What changes?
    AnswerThe temperature part of the acceleration factor drops from 347 to 28, so the median life in use is only about 32 years and the 0.1 % life about 6.9 years – the requirement is missed. A test result means little without the activation energy; it has to be measured at several temperatures.
  4. Up to which length is a copper line “immortal” at 1 MA/cm², and an aluminium line at 0.2 MA/cm²?
    AnswerL_c = (jL)_c/j: copper 3700/10^{6}\ \mathrm{cm} = 37\ \mathrm{\mu m}; aluminium 1260/(2\times10^{5})\ \mathrm{cm} = 63\ \mathrm{\mu m}.

Model & limitations

  • Black’s equation is an empirical extrapolation law. It assumes the same failure mechanism in the test and in use; if the mechanism changes between the two (another diffusion path, Joule heating of the test line, stress-induced voiding), the extrapolation is wrong. Joule heating is not modelled: the temperatures are those of the metal.
  • Activation energy and exponent are taken from the cited studies and held fixed for each metallisation. Real values depend on grain size and texture, alloying (copper in aluminium), cap and barrier layers, and line width (“bamboo” lines narrower than the grains behave differently). They must be measured for each technology.
  • Lognormal statistics with a single shape parameter σ, which you set – no typical value is implied. Real populations can be bimodal (for example early failures at vias). The 0.1 % quantile and the 10-year target are examples of a reliability requirement, not a standard.
  • Blech threshold: one value per metal from the cited measurements; published values for copper range from about 2000 to 10 000 A/cm depending on the dielectric and the cap, and the threshold depends on temperature and encapsulation. Below the threshold the page reports no failure, although real lines may still degrade slowly.
  • The picture of the line is schematic: void and hillock shapes, their positions and the moving electrons only illustrate the direction of the transport. The two plots are computed.
