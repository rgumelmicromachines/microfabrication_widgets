Lift-off: patterning without etching

The film is deposited over a patterned resist and the resist is then dissolved. The line comes out clean only if the film breaks at the resist edge, which depends on the resist profile and on the directions from which the atoms arrive.

Key equations

Resist of thickness h with sidewalls tilted by \gamma from the vertical (\gamma > 0: undercut). Atoms arrive along straight lines from the directions \theta_0 \pm \psi and stick where they land. A sidewall stays bare, and the film breaks there, when

\gamma \ge \psi + |\theta_0|, \qquad \tan\theta_0 = r/H

for wafer radius r under a source at distance H. The line on the substrate is shifted and the resist foot is set back by

\Delta x = -h\tan\theta_0, \qquad u = h\tan\gamma

A surface tilted by \chi against a parallel beam collects t\cos\chi/\cos\theta_0, so a sidewall with a positive slope |\gamma| under a beam along the normal gets t\sin|\gamma|. With arrival from the whole half-space (\psi = 90^\circ) the top of a vertical sidewall gets t/2 and the middle of an opening of width w gets t\,(w/2)/\sqrt{(w/2)^2 + h^2}.

Try it yourself

  1. A 100 mm wafer sits 40 cm above an evaporation source of 1 cm radius and carries 2 µm of resist. At the wafer edge, at which angle do the atoms arrive, how far is the line shifted, and how much undercut does a clean lift-off need?
    Answer\theta_0 = \arctan(5/40) = 7.1^\circ, shift 2\ \mathrm{\mu m}\cdot 0.125 = 0.25\ \mathrm{\mu m}. The source is seen under \psi = \arctan(1/40) = 1.4^\circ, so \gamma \ge 8.6^\circ, an undercut of u = 2\ \mathrm{\mu m}\cdot\tan 8.6^\circ = 0.30\ \mathrm{\mu m} at the substrate.
  2. A positive resist has sidewalls with a 10° positive slope. 300 nm of metal are evaporated along the wafer normal. How thick is the film on the sidewalls?
    Answert\sin|\gamma| = 300\ \mathrm{nm}\cdot\sin 10^\circ = 52\ \mathrm{nm}, a continuous film from the line to the top of the resist.
  3. A film is sputtered into a 4 µm opening in 1.5 µm of resist with vertical sidewalls. Assume arrival from the whole half-space. Which fraction of the nominal thickness reaches the middle of the opening and the top of the sidewall?
    AnswerMiddle: 2/\sqrt{2^2 + 1.5^2} = 0.80. Top of the sidewall: one half. The film is continuous over the resist edge.

Model & limitations

  • Two-dimensional cross-section of a long line with straight sidewalls. Atoms travel in straight lines and every atom sticks where it lands; re-emission and surface diffusion are left out. The cross-section and the film thicknesses are drawn to scale.
  • The arrival directions are spread evenly over ±ψ around θ₀. A small distant source is a narrow spread. ψ = 90° stands for sputtering as the limit without any preferred direction; real sputter tools lie between, depending on pressure, target distance and bias, and no measured angular distribution is used here.
  • The geometry does not change while the film grows. In reality the deposit on the resist edges grows sideways and narrows the opening, so thick films come out tapered. The page only reports when the film in the opening reaches the resist surface.
  • How thin a sidewall film must be for the solvent to get through is not modelled, and neither is the tearing of the film. The view after lift-off is schematic: it leaves every sidewall film standing as a fence.
  • Heating of the resist during deposition (softening and rounding of the profile, cross-linking, nitrogen bubbles in positive resists) is not modelled; it can spoil a profile that looks safe here.
  • No resist chemistry: the sidewall tilt is an input. Which exposure, bake and development give a certain undercut depends on the resist and is found in its data sheet.
