Etching silicon through a mask window

Crystal planes, selectivity and undercut: why the same window gives a V-groove, a bowl or a deep vertical trench.

Key relations

Anisotropic wet etching of (100) Si

KOH and TMAH etch {111} planes far more slowly than {100} planes. A window with edges along <110> therefore develops {111} sidewalls at

\theta = \arctan\sqrt{2} = 54.74^\circ, \qquad w_{bottom} = W - \sqrt{2}\,d, \qquad d_{max} = \frac{W}{\sqrt{2}}

Rates and selectivity

R(T) = R_\mathrm{ref}\,\exp\!\left[-\frac{E_a}{k}\left(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_\mathrm{ref}}\right)\right], \qquad S = \frac{R_\mathrm{Si}}{R_\mathrm{mask}}

The mask survives a time t_\mathrm{mask} = h_\mathrm{mask}/R_\mathrm{mask}.

Try it yourself

  1. A pressure-sensor membrane: the wafer is 380 µm thick and the membrane should be 20 µm. How long must you etch in 30 % KOH at 80 °C? What is the minimum window width so that the groove does not self-terminate before?
    AnswerDepth 360 µm at 1.1 µm/min ≈ 5.5 h. Self-termination at W/\sqrt{2} > 360\ \mathrm{\mu m} requires W > 509\ \mathrm{\mu m}; for a membrane of width a at the bottom, W = a + \sqrt{2}\cdot360\ \mathrm{\mu m}. With the {111} recession of this model (ratio 40) the opening also widens by about 11 µm per side during the 5.5 h – in a real design you would compensate for it.
  2. How thick must a thermal oxide mask be for the etch in task 1?
    Answer5.5 h × 7.7 nm/min ≈ 2.5 µm – impractical; that is why LPCVD nitride is used.
  3. In Explore mode, compare a 20 µm window etched 40 min in KOH and in DRIE. Why can KOH not make an 80 µm deep trench in a 20 µm window?
    AnswerKOH self-terminates at 20/\sqrt{2} \approx 14\ \mathrm{\mu m} (≈ 15–16 µm in the model, because the {111} walls also recede slowly). DRIE etches vertically regardless of crystal planes.

Model & limitations

  • 2-D cross-section of a long window whose edges are aligned to <110>. Misalignment, convex corners (which are strongly undercut in KOH) and the ends of the window are not modelled.
  • {111} planes etch at R_{100}/40 (KOH) or R_{100}/20 (TMAH). Published (100):(111) ratios span roughly 30 to 400 for KOH depending on concentration and temperature (Seidel: ~100 at 20 °C, ~30 at 100 °C) and 10–35 for TMAH, so the undercut shown here is on the high side.
  • Heavy boron doping stops KOH etching: above about 2×10¹⁹ cm⁻³ the rate falls roughly as the fourth power of the boron concentration, and a practical etch stop needs ≳ 5×10¹⁹–10²⁰ cm⁻³; the stop is weaker in concentrated KOH (Seidel et al. 1990, part II). It is used to make thin membranes. Doping is not modelled here.
  • Rates are constant in time: no loading effect, no aspect-ratio-dependent etching (ARDE) in DRIE, no hydrogen-bubble roughness. The temperature dependence uses one activation energy per material.
  • When the mask is consumed the drawing stops changing – in reality the whole surface would then be attacked.
  • DRIE scallops and the mask thickness are drawn larger than real for visibility.
