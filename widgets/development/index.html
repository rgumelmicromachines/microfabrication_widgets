Development: from exposure dose to resist profile

The exposure only changes the chemistry of the resist. The developer turns that change into a profile – how much dose and how much time does a line of the right width need?

Key equations

Exposure (Dill)

With m the fraction of the photoactive compound (PAC) that is still intact and I the intensity at depth z:

\frac{\partial I}{\partial z} = -(A\,m + B)\,I, \qquad \frac{\partial m}{\partial t} = -C\,I\,m

At the surface this gives m = e^{-C E} for the dose E. A is the absorption that bleaches away with the PAC, B the absorption that stays, C the sensitivity. If the substrate reflects nothing, the profile in depth follows from one equation:

\frac{dm}{dz} = m\,\bigl[A\,(1-m) - B \ln m\bigr], \qquad m(0) = e^{-C E}

Front, dose to clear and contrast

The developer front moves along its normal at the local rate. In a large open area it reaches the depth z after

T(z) = \int_0^{z} \frac{dz'}{r\bigl(m(z')\bigr)}, \qquad T(d) = t \ \Rightarrow\ E = D_c

and the dose for which the whole thickness d is developed in the time t is the dose to clear D_c. The contrast is the slope of the thickness left against the logarithm of the dose:

\gamma = \frac{1}{\log_{10}(D_c / D_0)}, \qquad \text{dark erosion} = r_{min}\,t

Try it yourself

  1. Which dose at 365 nm leaves half of the PAC at the surface of an AZ 1500 resist? How much is left after 100 mJ/cm²?
    AnswerE = \ln 2 / C = 0.693/0.0239 = 29\ \mathrm{mJ/cm^2}. After 100 mJ/cm²: m = e^{-2.39} = 0.092.
  2. An unexposed 1.2 µm film absorbs with A + B, a fully bleached one with B only. Which fraction of the 365 nm light reaches the substrate in each case?
    AnswerUnexposed: e^{-(1.0133+0.2177)\cdot 1.2} = 0.23. Bleached: e^{-0.2177\cdot 1.2} = 0.77. The film becomes more transparent while it is exposed, so the bottom catches up – but it always lags behind the surface.
  3. Use the 70 °C parameter set. Calculate a and the development rate for m = 0.3, m = 0.61 and m = 0.8.
    Answera = \tfrac{7}{5}\cdot 0.39^6 = 0.0049. Rates: 53 nm/s, 23 nm/s and 0.86 nm/s. Between m = 0.8 and m = 0.61 the rate changes 27-fold: the resist behaves almost like a switch.
  4. How much unexposed resist do the two parameter sets remove in a 60 s development, and what is the ratio r_{max}/r_{min}?
    Answer70 °C: 0.15\cdot 60 = 9\ \mathrm{nm}, ratio 367. 100 °C: 2\cdot 60 = 120\ \mathrm{nm}, ratio 10.5.
  5. In Explore mode (1.2 µm, 70 °C set, 60 s) read D_0 and D_c from the contrast curve and calculate γ. Then set the thickness to 2 µm. What happens to the dose to clear?
    AnswerD_0 = 14, D_c = 44\ \mathrm{mJ/cm^2}, \gamma = 1/\log_{10}(44/14) = 2.1. For 2 µm the dose to clear rises to 85 mJ/cm²: the film is thicker and its bottom receives less light.
  6. A wafer with an aluminium film is developed for 90 s in 0.26 N TMAH. The resist in the open areas is gone after 25 s. How much aluminium is removed according to the two quoted etch rates?
    AnswerThe aluminium is exposed for 65 s. At 10 nm/min: 11 nm. At 70 nm/min: 76 nm. The two manufacturer documents disagree by a factor of seven, so the loss has to be measured on the film in question.

Model & limitations

  • Two resists in one model. The Dill parameters are those of the AZ 1500 series at 365 nm. The development-rate parameters were measured by Mack for a different, older resist, AZ 1350J, in AZ Developer diluted 1:1. No published Mack parameter set for an AZ 1500 resist was found, so the doses and times of this page show the behaviour of a DNQ/novolak resist and are not a process recipe for AZ 1500.
  • No dark-erosion value for AZ 1500 was found in the manufacturer documents. The page uses r_{min} of the AZ 1350J sets.
  • The choice of developer changes only the attack on aluminium. Its effect on the development rate (normality, dilution, temperature, agitation, an inhibited surface layer) is not modelled, because no rate parameters are published for the other developers.
  • Attack on aluminium: about 10 nm/min for 0.26 N TMAH in one manufacturer document and about 70 nm/min in another; both bounds are shown. For NaOH- and KOH-based developers only “several 10 to over 100 nm/min” is quoted; the page uses 100 nm/min as a reference rate. The recess is drawn for the higher rate.
  • The substrate reflects nothing: no standing waves and no extra dose from the reflection. Silicon reflects roughly 30 % and a metal film more than 90 % of the exposure light (MicroChemicals), so real profiles on aluminium show standing waves and need less dose.
  • The dose is the dose at 365 nm. A mercury lamp also exposes the resist at 405 and 436 nm, where the Dill parameters differ.
  • The image at the mask edge is the scalar contact/proximity image of the lithography lesson, evaluated in the middle of the resist and taken as constant through its thickness apart from absorption.
  • The front is computed on a grid of 41 × 33 points with a first-order method; widths are good to a few per cent of the line width. The contrast is the slope of the calculated curve at the dose to clear, which is steeper than a straight-line fit through measured points.
  • The cross-section is to scale. The mask is drawn above it only to show where the chrome edges are.
