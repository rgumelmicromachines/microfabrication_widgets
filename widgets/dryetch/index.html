Dry etching: anisotropy, bias and end point

Ions etch straight down, radicals etch in every direction. How wide does the opening come out, and how do you know when to stop?

Key relations

Anisotropy and bias

With a vertical (ion-driven) etch rate R_v and a lateral (chemical) rate R_l:

A = 1 - \frac{R_l}{R_v}, \qquad U = R_l\,t, \qquad B \equiv d_f - d_m = 2U

Complete anisotropic etching: A = 1,\ B = 0. Complete isotropic etching: A = 0 and, at the end point, B = 2h_f.

End point and overetch

t_c = \frac{h_f}{R_v}, \qquad \text{overetch} = \frac{t - t_c}{t_c}, \qquad \text{Si recess} = \frac{R_v}{S_{\mathrm{SiO_2:Si}}}\,(t - t_c)

Laser interferometry

The beam reflected from the film surface and from the film/substrate interface interferes; each removed thickness

\Delta d = \frac{\lambda}{2n} = \frac{632.8\ \mathrm{nm}}{2\cdot 1.457} = 217\ \mathrm{nm}

gives one full fringe (SiO₂ at the He–Ne wavelength). The fringe period in time, \Delta t = \Delta d / R_v, measures the etch rate in situ; the end of the oscillation marks the end point.

Try it yourself

  1. 500 nm SiO₂, R_v = 50 nm/min, R_l = 5 nm/min, mask opening 1 µm, 12 min etch, selectivity SiO₂ : Si = 20. When is the end point, how much overetch is that, and what are the bias and the Si recess?
    Answert_c = 500/50 = 10\ \mathrm{min}, overetch 20 %. U = 5\cdot 12 = 60\ \mathrm{nm}, B = 120\ \mathrm{nm}, etched opening 1.12 µm. Recess 50/20 \cdot 2 = 5\ \mathrm{nm}. Check it in Explore mode.
  2. You count the fringes of the laser signal while etching 800 nm of oxide. How many do you see, and what is the etch rate if one fringe takes 2.2 min?
    AnswerN = 2nh_f/\lambda = 2\cdot1.457\cdot800/632.8 \approx 3.7 fringes. R_v = 217\ \mathrm{nm} / 2.2\ \mathrm{min} \approx 99\ \mathrm{nm/min}.
  3. Why does a longer overetch widen the etched opening even when the substrate is barely etched?
    AnswerThe lateral (chemical) component keeps attacking the sidewalls of the film for as long as the plasma is on: U = R_l t grows with the total time, not only until the end point.

Model & limitations

  • Geometric etch-front model: a constant directional rate in the open area plus a constant isotropic rate on every surface from the moment it is uncovered, which gives a rounded top and a straight, tapered wall. Real profiles also show tapering by sidewall passivation, bowing, trenching at the foot, faceting of the mask and microloading; none of these are modelled.
  • Etch rates and selectivities are free parameters. The defaults are illustrative values for oxide etching in a fluorocarbon RIE; real values depend on gas mixture, pressure, power and tool and must be measured.
  • The resist erodes only vertically and uniformly; a lost mask is reported but the subsequent attack of the film is not drawn.
  • End-point signal: ideal normal-incidence reflectance of a flat SiO₂ layer on Si (no roughness, no beam divergence, no change of the optical constants during etching). Real signals are noisier and their amplitude varies.
  • The reactor and the particle motion are schematic; the cross-section of the feature is to scale.
