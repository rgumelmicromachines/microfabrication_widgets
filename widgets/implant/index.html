Ion implantation: how deep, how much, and what stops it?

Dopant ions are fired into the wafer with a chosen energy and an exactly counted dose. Energy sets the depth, dose sets the concentration – and the mask must catch the tail.

Key equations

As-implanted profile in an amorphous target (Gaussian approximation) with projected range R_p, straggle \Delta R_p and dose Q:

N(x) = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Delta R_p}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-R_p)^2}{2\,\Delta R_p^2}\right], \qquad N_p = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Delta R_p} \approx \frac{0.4\,Q}{\Delta R_p}

Junction(s) where the implanted dopant equals the substrate doping N_B:

x_j = R_p \pm \Delta R_p\,\sqrt{2\ln(N_p/N_B)}

A mask of thickness d (range R_p^*, straggle \Delta R_p^* in the mask material) lets through the fraction T of the dose; it is sufficient when the concentration at the mask/Si interface stays below N_B:

T = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}\,\frac{d - R_p^*}{\sqrt2\,\Delta R_p^*}, \qquad d \ge R_p^* + \Delta R_p^*\sqrt{2\ln(N_p^*/N_B)}

The dose is the collected charge: Q = I\,t/(n\,q\,A) for ions of charge state n on the area A.

Try it yourself

  1. Boron, 50 keV, 10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}}, into n-type Si with N_B = 10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}} (R_p = 174.6 nm, \Delta R_p = 64.2 nm). Peak concentration and junction depth?
    AnswerN_p = 10^{15}/(2.507\cdot 64.2\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{cm}) = 6.2\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}. x_j = 174.6 + 64.2\sqrt{2\ln(6200)} = 174.6 + 64.2\cdot4.18 \approx 443\ \mathrm{nm}. Check it in Explore mode.
  2. Phosphorus, 100 keV, 10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}}, into p-type Si with N_B = 10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}. How thick must the photoresist mask be (R_p^* = 241 nm, \Delta R_p^* = 49 nm)? Compare with the design rule R_p^* + 5\Delta R_p^*.
    AnswerN_p^* = 8.1\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}, \sqrt{2\ln(8100)} = 4.24, so d \ge 241 + 4.24\cdot49 \approx 449\ \mathrm{nm}. The rule R_p^* + 5\Delta R_p^* = 486\ \mathrm{nm} adds a small margin; real resist masks are usually much thicker (≥ 1 µm), because the resist must also survive the process.
  3. An arsenic source/drain implant of 5\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}} on a 200 mm wafer with a 10 mA beam of As⁺: how long does it take?
    Answert = Q\,q\,A/I = 5\times10^{15}\cdot1.602\times10^{-19}\cdot314/0.01 \approx 25\ \mathrm{s} (plus the time the beam spends off the wafer while scanning).

Model & limitations

  • Gaussian profile from the first two moments (R_p, \Delta R_p) for an amorphous target. Real profiles are skewed (boron above about 100 keV has a pronounced asymmetry) and are described by Pearson-IV distributions with higher moments.
  • No channeling: in crystalline Si some ions travel far along open crystal directions and form a tail. Wafers are therefore usually implanted tilted by about 7° (common industrial practice; Cho 1985).
  • As implanted: no damage, no amorphisation and no anneal. An anneal is needed to repair the lattice and activate the dopants; it also moves the profile (diffusion, including transient enhanced diffusion).
  • The profile under a too-thin mask is computed with the ranges of the mask material all the way, i.e. the slightly different stopping in the Si below is ignored.
  • Range data: SRIM-2013 tables for Si; for SiO₂ and photoresist (AZ-111), values read from published SRIM-2013 graphs (±2 nm in R_p). Energies 10–200 keV, interpolated log–log. The lateral spread under the mask edge is drawn schematically (about \Delta R_p).
  • Dose ranges quoted in the tour (about 10¹¹–10¹³ cm⁻² for threshold adjustment, up to about 10¹⁶ cm⁻² for source/drain and polysilicon doping) follow Rubin & Poate (2003).
