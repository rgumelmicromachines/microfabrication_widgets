Microfabrication Interactive Process game

Lithography run: aluminium meander resistor

You get a wafer and a work order. Eight stations later an electrical test decides whether the resistor is on target. No setting is blocked: a wrong one shows up later, under the microscope or at the test. The work order is under the figure.

Work order

Pattern the aluminium film into a meander resistor whose two pads land on the contact windows that are already in the oxide. The job takes one mask and one lithography level, and the film is etched wet.

Wafer
100 mm silicon, oxide with 10 µm contact windows, 69 dies
Film
500 nm evaporated aluminium at the wafer centre, about 5 % thinner at the edge
Mask
Soda-lime glass; meander of 5 µm lines and 5 µm spaces, 60 mm long; pads overlap the windows by 2 µm; two alignment marks 60 mm apart
Target
795 Ω per resistor (4.0 µm electrical line width), test limit ±10 %
Resist
AZ 1500 series, 1.3–1.6 µm after soft bake
Tools
Spin coater, hotplate, mask aligner with dose control (8.5 mW/cm² at 365 nm, hard contact or proximity), developer bench, aluminium etchant at 25 or 40 °C, microscope
Documents
AZ 1500 series data sheet (Merck, PDF at microchemicals.com) · aluminium etchant Type A (Transene)

You can strip the resist and coat again at any time before the etch; after the etch only a new wafer helps.

After the test a report appears under the figure. It lists your settings, the outcome of each station and the score, and it can be printed or saved.

Plan before you run

  1. Check the target resistance of the work order. Use the bulk resistivity of aluminium, \rho = 2.65\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\,\mathrm{m}.
    AnswerR = \rho L/(w h) = 2.65\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.06 / (4\times10^{-6}\cdot 5\times10^{-7}) = 795\ \Omega. The sheet resistance is \rho/h = 53\ \mathrm{m\Omega/\square} and the meander has 15 000 squares.
  2. The lamp delivers 8.5 mW/cm² at 365 nm. How long does the aligner expose for a dose of 128 mJ/cm²?
    Answer128\ \mathrm{mJ/cm^2} / 8.5\ \mathrm{mW/cm^2} = 15\ \mathrm{s}.
  3. An etchant removes 80 Å/s. How long does it take to clear 500 nm at the wafer centre, and the 473 nm at the edge?
    Answer80 Å/s = 480 nm/min. Centre: 500/480 = 62.5\ \mathrm{s}. Edge: 59 s. Every second after that is overetch.
  4. The etch is isotropic. A 5.0 µm resist line covers the 500 nm film. What is the mean width of the aluminium line when the film has just cleared, and after 20 % more etch time?
    AnswerThe wall is a quarter circle of radius r around the resist edge. Just cleared, r = h = 0.5\ \mathrm{\mu m}: the mean undercut per side is \pi r/4 = 0.39\ \mathrm{\mu m}, mean width 4.21 µm. With r = 0.6\ \mathrm{\mu m} the mean undercut over the film thickness is 0.52 µm per side and the width 3.96 µm. That is where the 4.0 µm of the work order comes from.
  5. The soda-lime mask and the wafer are 1.5 K warmer than at the first exposure. How far off is a die 45 mm from the centre after perfect alignment, and how much of the 2 µm pad overlap is left for the alignment itself?
    AnswerM = (9.06 - 2.6)\cdot 1.5 = 9.7\ \mathrm{ppm}, at 45 mm 0.44 µm. About 1.5 µm is left.
  6. A four-point probe on the unpatterned film reads V/I = 11.7\ \mathrm{m\Omega}. What is the sheet resistance?
    AnswerR_s = (\pi/\ln 2)\cdot V/I = 4.532 \cdot 11.7 = 53\ \mathrm{m\Omega/\square}.

Model, rules & limitations

  • Each station runs the model of its lesson: soft bake, lithography resolution, development, alignment and overlay, evaporation, dry etching (its etch front with equal rates in all directions) and the four-point probe. The limitations listed on those pages apply here too.
  • The resist model is a stand-in. The Dill parameters are those of AZ 1500 at 365 nm, but the development-rate parameters were measured for AZ 1350J in AZ Developer 1:1 (Mack 1987); none are published for AZ 1500. The doses and times that work in the game are therefore not a recipe for the real resist. The same rate parameters are used for every developer.
  • Spin curve. The thickness is read from the graph of the current data sheet (accuracy about ±0.1 µm, 150 mm wafers) and interpolated between 1000 and 4000 rpm. An older data sheet of the same series gives somewhat different numbers, for example 1.20 µm instead of 1.35 µm for AZ 1512 HS at 4000 rpm. Your answer is accepted within 0.15 µm or 10 %.
  • Rule for the soft bake. A film that holds more solvent than the measured film of the soft-bake lesson after 90 °C for 50 s, the mildest bake in the manufacturer documents, sticks to the mask and the pattern is lost. The threshold is a rule of the game; the manufacturer notes name sticking as a consequence of too much solvent without giving a number. Photoactive compound that decomposes in the bake is counted as lost for the exposure.
  • Hard bake. Positive resist starts to cross-link above about 140 °C, and solvents reach their limit as strippers from hard-bake temperatures of about 150–160 °C (MicroChemicals). In the game a hard bake at 150 °C or more leaves the pads covered and no die makes contact. The same note calls the resists stable in the etchant “at least after a sufficient hardbake”, without a number, so skipping the bake has no effect in the game.
  • Developer on aluminium. AZ Developer is made for minimal attack on aluminium; no rate is published and the game takes it as zero. 0.26 N TMAH: 10 nm/min from the Merck data sheet (the MicroChemicals product page quotes about 70 nm/min). NaOH- and KOH-based developers: 100 nm/min as a reference rate. The attack acts on the spaces, which are etched anyway, and sideways under the resist edge by the same amount, so its effect on the resistor is small.
  • Etch. Rates of 30 Å/s at 25 °C and 80 Å/s at 40 °C from the manufacturer of the etchant; real baths age and hydrogen bubbles make the etch uneven. The etch is taken as ideally isotropic and uniform, and the resist as unaffected by it.
  • Resistance. Calculated with the bulk resistivity of aluminium, 2.650 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. A thin film has a higher resistivity because of its grain boundaries and surfaces; no thin-film value was verified, so every resistance here is a lower bound. Contact resistance is not modelled.
  • Proximity printing is limited to gaps of 10–30 µm. The image model uses an ideal grating and one wavelength and would show sharp images again at certain larger gaps (Talbot effect), which broadband light does not.
  • Scenario. Wafer, film, mask layout, temperature offset, test limit and scoring are chosen for the game. The film thickness across the wafer follows the cosine law for a small planar source 30 cm away. The mask sits up to 3 µm and 120 µrad off before alignment, at random for each wafer.
  • The top view is what a microscope would show at the wafer centre, with a simplified layout: eight runs instead of the full meander, drawn with equal lines and spaces. The pads, their offset against the contact windows and the detail of three lines are to scale. The cross-section is drawn with an enlarged vertical scale and a schematic oxide thickness; after exposure it shows the latent image, which cannot be seen on a real wafer. The alignment marks are an example, to scale in their 20 µm frames: a chrome cross 2 µm wide on the mask inside the outline of a cross 4 µm wide that the layer below left in the film.
